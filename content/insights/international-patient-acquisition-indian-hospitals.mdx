India receives an estimated **600,000+ international medical travellers a year** across the major destination cities — Delhi NCR, Chennai, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Hyderabad, Kochi (industry tracking estimates; the official MoT figures count e-Medical Visa arrivals which run lower). The economic value is meaningful, the clinical capability is genuine, and yet most Indian hospitals chasing international patients waste their budget on generic Google Ads campaigns targeting "best hospital India."

The reason is segmentation. A patient flying from Dhaka for a knee replacement is a fundamentally different acquisition problem than a patient flying from Lagos for cardiac surgery, who is again different from a UAE patient seeking a second opinion on an oncology diagnosis. Same hospital, same OPD, four entirely different marketing funnels.

This is the country-by-country, channel-by-channel, ethics-compliant playbook for international patient acquisition that we've watched separate the serious players from the brochure-and-prayer crowd.

## The Four Source Segments

### South Asia: Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Nepal

The highest-volume segment for most Indian hospitals, especially in Kolkata, Chennai and [Delhi NCR](/delhi-ncr). High patient flow, lower revenue per case, often relationship- and agent-driven rather than digital-driven.

Key dynamics:

- **Bangladesh accounts for the majority of South Asian inbound** — patients cross via land borders (Petrapole-Benapole, Hili) on M-visas. Specialties: cardiology, oncology, gastroenterology, hepatology, transplants.
- **Sri Lankan patients** typically fly into Chennai or Bengaluru. Strong oncology and orthopaedic demand.
- **Maldivian patients** are partially government-funded under the Aasandha scheme, with formal hospital empanelment processes.
- **Nepali patients** cross into UP, Bihar and Uttarakhand for routine and specialty care.

Marketing channels that work: Facebook (very high penetration in Bangladesh), WhatsApp groups for diaspora coordinators, agent and referral networks, regional language content (Bangla pages on your site materially outperform English-only).

### Middle East and GCC: UAE, Oman, Saudi, Iraq, Yemen

Higher revenue per case, expectations closer to Western private healthcare, longer pre-arrival research cycles. Patients often have insurance coverage from home country or are paying privately at a premium tier.

Dynamics:

- **Arabic-language content is non-negotiable.** A patient researching cardiac surgery from Riyadh will read three hospital websites in Arabic before considering the one that only offers English.
- **Doctor introductions matter.** Many GCC patients come on referrals from a doctor at home — the international marketing team's job is to enable that relationship, not replace it.
- **Direct flights and visa-on-arrival** for GCC nationals shorten the decision cycle. Patients increasingly compare India against Thailand and Turkey on a 2–4 week decision horizon.

Marketing channels: Arabic [SEO](/seo), Arabic Google Ads, Instagram (high penetration in UAE, Saudi), doctor-to-doctor introduction programmes, embassy and consulate liaison.

### Africa: Nigeria, Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Francophone Africa

Strong and growing segment. East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia) leans toward Mumbai, Bengaluru and Chennai. West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana) and Francophone Africa (DRC, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal) increasingly drive volume into Delhi NCR and Mumbai.

Dynamics:

- **Cardiac, oncology, transplant and orthopaedic surgery** drive most of the volume. Cost differentials versus South Africa or European destinations are substantial.
- **Payment structures vary** — government-funded for some Nigerian state government referrals, private-pay for most others, occasional NGO and missionary referral chains.
- **Visa, accommodation and translation** are bigger barriers than clinical capability. The hospital that nails the operational support wins.

Channels: Google search (high intent), French-language content for Francophone Africa, doctor exchange programmes, structured agent networks with verified credentials.

### CIS and Central Asia: Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia

Smaller in volume but growing, particularly in Delhi NCR and Mumbai. Russian-language content and Russian-speaking IPD coordinators are a meaningful differentiator. Cosmetic, orthopaedic and cardiac specialties dominate.

## The Visa Mechanics

The marketing team needs to understand the visa process because patients ask, and a hospital that can answer with precision converts faster.

The relevant categories:

- **e-Medical Visa (e-MED)** — online application, valid for treatment at a recognised hospital, currently allows multiple entries within a defined window.
- **Medical Visa (M-visa)** — issued by Indian missions abroad, longer validity for extended treatment.
- **Medical Attendant Visa (MED-X)** — for accompanying family members; typically two attendants per patient.

Your IPD desk should be able to:

- Issue a pro-forma invoice with treatment summary on hospital letterhead — required for visa application
- Issue an invitation letter signed by the treating doctor
- Coordinate with the patient's nearest Indian mission for visa appointment scheduling
- Provide post-arrival visa extension support if treatment extends

Hospitals with NABH and ideally JCI accreditation get on the Ministry's recognised list, which streamlines patient visa applications.

## Channel Strategy by Region

The mistake hospitals make is running a single international campaign across all source markets. The unit economics break down. Each region needs a separate channel mix:

### Bangladesh

- **Facebook + Messenger** — primary discovery channel. Bangla-language video content showing patient journeys.
- **WhatsApp** — communication once enquiry is made. Most Bangladeshi patients prefer WhatsApp over email.
- **Agent-led referrals** — established travel agents and medical coordinators in Dhaka, Chittagong, Sylhet. The hospital should formalise these relationships with clear referral protocols, not informal commission structures.
- **In-country camps** — periodic OPD camps in Dhaka and Chittagong, ideally hosted in partnership with a local hospital or clinic.

### GCC

- **Arabic SEO and content** — landing pages in Arabic for cardiac surgery, oncology, IVF, orthopaedics. [Cardiology](/specialties/cardiology) and [oncology](/specialties/oncology) translated, with Arabic-speaking patient coordinator contact prominent.
- **Google Ads in Arabic** — far less competition than English; CPCs materially lower.
- **Doctor-to-doctor introductions** — invite GCC specialists for case discussions, observerships. Build the professional network.
- **Embassy liaison** — for Iraq, Yemen, some other markets, the Indian embassy facilitates patient introductions.

### Africa

- **Google Search** — high-intent queries like "best cardiac hospital in India for African patients" or "cancer treatment India cost" still drive a meaningful share of African inbound.
- **French content for Francophone Africa** — even basic French translations of key procedure pages outperform English-only.
- **Verified agent network** — Africa has more agent fraud risk than other regions. Hospitals serious about Africa run formal accreditation of in-country coordinators.
- **WhatsApp and direct phone** — patients want voice contact before committing.

### CIS

- **Russian-language content and IPD coordinator** — non-negotiable for any serious CIS programme.
- **Local agent partnerships** in Tashkent, Almaty, Bishkek.

## The IPD Desk Function

The international patient desk is not a marketing function. It's a service operation that determines whether your marketing actually converts. The structure that works:

- **Dedicated coordinators by region or language** — Arabic-speaking, French-speaking, Bangla-speaking, Russian-speaking. Not one generalist trying to cover all geographies.
- **24/7 enquiry response** — international patients call at all hours given time zone differences. A 12-hour response window loses the patient to a competitor.
- **End-to-end support**: visa documentation, airport pickup, accommodation booking near the hospital, in-hospital coordination, translation services during consultations, billing assistance, post-discharge logistics.
- **Embassy liaison** for specific country needs (Iraq, Yemen, parts of Africa).
- **Quote turnaround SLA** — package estimates within 24 hours for routine procedures, 48 hours for complex multi-modality treatment plans.

Hospitals running this well have international patient revenue at 15–25% of overall IPD revenue. Hospitals running it poorly have international patients as 2–5% of revenue with high complaint rates.

## NABH International and JCI

Two accreditation marquees matter for international patient marketing:

- **NABH International** — the international-patient-focused programme of the National Accreditation Board, with explicit focus on international patient services, transparent pricing, and outcomes reporting.
- **JCI (Joint Commission International)** — the US-based gold-standard accreditation, expensive to obtain and maintain, but the strongest credibility marker for Western and Western-influenced markets (GCC, parts of Africa, Russia).

For a serious international patient strategy targeting GCC or beyond, at least NABH International is table stakes. JCI is differentiation. Both require multi-year operational investment, not just paperwork.

## Pricing Transparency

International patients price-compare more aggressively than domestic patients. A hospital that responds to a cardiac surgery enquiry with "please come for consultation, we will discuss cost" is at a structural disadvantage against a hospital that responds with a clear package quote.

The minimum the patient wants to see:

- **Procedure package cost** — surgery fees, anaesthesia, hospital stay, ICU days, post-op consults
- **What's included vs. excluded** — implants, blood products, complications, extended ICU
- **Estimated total stay** in hospital and in India (including pre-op tests and post-op recovery)
- **Accommodation cost estimate** for attendant
- **Currency** quoted in USD typically, with INR equivalent

The package model isn't perfect — complications and complexity add cost. But a transparent package with explicit exclusions converts far better than "depends on the case."

A video consultation pathway — a 30-minute paid second opinion with the treating surgeon over Zoom or WhatsApp Video before the patient flies — has become the highest-conversion step in international patient marketing for procedural specialties. It de-risks the trip for the patient and lets your surgeon scope the case.

## Compliance and Ethics

International patient marketing operates in a more ambiguous regulatory space than domestic marketing — but the boundaries matter, both legally and reputationally.

The lines:

- **NMC restrictions on commissions to non-medical referrers** — domestic regulation that increasingly applies to international agent commissions. Hospitals should structure agent relationships as legitimate marketing service contracts with deliverables, not per-patient commissions to non-medical persons.
- **No guarantees of outcome** — ASCI rules apply to your hospital's marketing regardless of where the patient is reading it from. "Guaranteed cure" or "100% success rate" claims are not acceptable.
- **Transparent pricing** — required by NABH International standards.
- **DPDP Act and cross-border data** — international patient data is subject to [DPDP](/glossary) requirements when processed in India. Cross-border data transfers for follow-up care need lawful basis.
- **Avoid "medical tourism" framing** for clinical content — the framing trivialises serious treatment. "International patient services" or "medical value travel" reads better and aligns with how patients self-describe.

## Post-Treatment Continuity

The international patient relationship doesn't end at discharge. The hospital that handles post-treatment well gets repeat business and referrals; the hospital that loses contact gets blamed for any complication.

The continuity stack:

- **Discharge summary in English, plus translation** for non-English markets
- **Scheduled telemedicine follow-ups** at 2 weeks, 1 month, 3 months
- **Home-country specialist coordination** — your surgeon connects with the patient's local cardiologist or oncologist to hand off ongoing management
- **Medication continuity** — written protocols the patient can give to their local pharmacist
- **Emergency contact** — a named coordinator the patient can WhatsApp if they have concerns

This is operational work, not marketing. But it's the single biggest driver of word-of-mouth referrals from the international patient community — and word-of-mouth is the highest-converting channel in international patient acquisition by a wide margin.

## What This Compounds Into

A serious international patient programme is a 24–36 month build. The hospitals that started in 2020–22 are now seeing 20%+ of revenue from international patients, with stable referral networks, established embassy relationships, and a coordinator team that's been together long enough to know the cases by name.

The hospitals starting in 2026 are entering a more competitive market — but the demand is also growing, and the channel mix is now well-understood enough that the build can be more disciplined than it was five years ago.
