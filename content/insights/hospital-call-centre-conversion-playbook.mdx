A 400-bed multispecialty hospital in Bengaluru spends ₹52 lakh a month on marketing. The CMO ships campaigns across Google, Meta, YouTube, hoardings on ORR. Calls come in at roughly 6,000 a month. The marketing team reports the funnel as: 6,000 calls → 4,200 qualified → 1,680 appointments booked → 1,008 OPD shows.

That 40% call-to-appointment rate is industry-typical. It's also the single biggest leak in the entire patient acquisition pipeline. **Moving that one number from 40% to 65% is worth more than doubling the marketing budget** — and costs roughly ₹8 lakh in training, scripting and tooling. Yet most hospitals will spend the next quarter debating creative for the next campaign instead.

The most leveraged intervention in hospital marketing isn't a new ad campaign. It's fixing the call centre.

## What Bad Looks Like

We've mystery-shopped over 200 Indian hospital call centres in the last three years. The pattern of failure is depressingly consistent:

- **Rings 5–8 times.** Sometimes routes to voicemail. Sometimes a recorded "all our agents are busy" loop.
- **Agent has no specialty context.** Patient says "I need a cardiologist." Agent asks "for which doctor?" Patient doesn't know — that's why they called.
- **No same-day slot offered.** "We have an appointment next Tuesday at 4pm." When the patient asks for earlier, agent says "let me check and call back." Doesn't call back.
- **No qualification.** Agent doesn't ask the patient's location, insurance status, age, or symptom severity. Routes a 78-year-old with chest pain to a general OPD slot two days out.
- **Manual appointment book.** Agent flips through a notebook. No real-time sync with the doctor's calendar. Double-bookings discovered the next morning.
- **No WhatsApp confirm.** No SMS reminder. No-show rates climb because the patient half-remembers the appointment and forgets the time.

Every one of these failures is fixable. Most are fixable in under 90 days. The reason they persist is that nobody in the org chart owns the number — marketing thinks the call centre is operations, operations thinks the call centre is admin, and the call centre supervisor reports to whoever happens to have a free chair on the floor.

## The First-Call Appointment Rule

The discipline that anchors a high-performing call centre is one operating rule:

> Every qualified inbound call must end with either a **confirmed appointment slot** or a **clear next-action with a committed timeline** (specialist callback within 4 hours, IPD coordinator visit within 24 hours, financial counselling appointment within 48 hours).

No "we'll call you back." No "let me check and revert." Either the call ends with a slot, or the call ends with a named human and a named hour.

This single rule, enforced, takes most hospitals from 40% to 55% conversion in a quarter. The remaining 10 percentage points come from the scripting, training and technology stack discussed below.

## The Scripting Framework

A good script isn't a rigid teleprompter. It's a structure with mandatory elements and flexible filler. The three-act framework:

### Act 1: Verify and Qualify (45 seconds)

- "Good morning, this is [Name] from [Hospital]. May I have your name and a mobile number where I can reach you?"
- "Are you calling for yourself or for a family member?"
- "Could you tell me briefly what's troubling [you/the patient]?"
- "Where are you located? We have campuses at [list]."
- "Have you visited us before?"

Outcome of Act 1: agent knows who's calling, for whom, with what condition, from where, and whether they're an existing patient (which triggers MRN lookup).

### Act 2: Match and Recommend (60–90 seconds)

This is where most call centres fail. The agent needs a specialty-matching matrix on screen — symptom → specialty → recommended doctor. A patient saying "knee pain since 6 months, can't climb stairs" should be matched to orthopaedic OPD, with a knee specialist preferred over general ortho, with a sub-specialty in joint replacement if age > 55.

> The matrix doesn't need to be sophisticated. A laminated A4 sheet works. But it must exist, and agents must be trained on it weekly.

The agent then recommends: "Based on what you've described, I'd suggest you see Dr. [Name], our knee specialist. He has slots tomorrow at 11am or 4pm, or Thursday morning. Which works better?"

### Act 3: Close and Confirm (45 seconds)

- Confirm the slot
- Confirm the campus and exact location ("third floor, OPD-3, ask for the orthopaedic reception")
- Confirm what the patient should bring (previous reports, MRI films, insurance card)
- Quote consultation fee
- Send WhatsApp confirmation with location pin while still on the call
- "You'll get an SMS reminder 24 hours before. If anything changes, please call us back on this same number."

End-to-end call: under 3 minutes for a clean booking. Longer for complex cases that need IPD coordinator involvement.

## The Four Call Categories

Not every call is the same. Train agents on four distinct workflows:

### 1. New Enquiry (60–70% of volume)

The default flow described above. Goal: book OPD slot or specialist consult.

### 2. Existing Patient (15–20% of volume)

MRN lookup first. Pull treatment history before the conversation gets technical. These calls often relate to follow-up appointments, prescription refills, or report queries — different routing than new enquiries.

### 3. Emergency or Triage (5–10% of volume)

The highest-stakes category. Agent must triage in under 30 seconds: chest pain + sweating + radiating to arm → don't book OPD, route to emergency dispatch and instruct to come immediately or call ambulance. **Every agent needs scripted triage criteria** for the top 15 emergency presentations. Get clinical sign-off on the script. This is non-negotiable.

### 4. Information Request (10% of volume)

"What does an MRI cost?" "Do you accept CGHS?" "Where exactly is your Sarjapur campus?" These are not appointment bookings, but they're qualified interest. Agent should answer the question, *then* attempt to convert: "Would you like to book a consultation while you're on the call?"

## Training Cadence

The skill atrophies without continuous reinforcement. The cadence that holds:

- **Weekly:** 30-minute team review of 5 actual call recordings. Two good, three with issues. Group debrief on what worked.
- **Bi-weekly:** Specialty deep-dives. Cardiology week, oncology week, orthopaedics week. The relevant doctor or department head spends 45 minutes explaining the patient journey, common questions, and what they want the agent to know.
- **Monthly:** Mystery shopper calls — your own team calls the call centre posing as patients. Score on the scripted criteria.
- **Quarterly:** Scenario role-plays. Difficult patients, escalations, second-opinion requests, international enquiries.
- **Per-incident:** Any complaint or missed conversion gets reviewed within 48 hours.

The agents who improve fastest are the ones who hear their own recordings within 24 hours of a call. The compounding effect is significant — a 3% monthly improvement gets a team from 40% to 65% conversion in roughly 18 months.

## The Technology Stack

You don't need a Salesforce Health Cloud licence to run a good call centre — but you do need basic infrastructure. The minimum viable stack:

- **CRM with specialty mapping.** LeadSquared, Zoho, Freshsales, or custom — but it must let the agent see the symptom → specialty → doctor matrix on screen.
- **Call recording.** Every call recorded, retrievable by call ID. Critical for training and dispute resolution.
- **Integrated appointment system.** Real-time doctor calendar visibility. No more flipping through notebooks.
- **WhatsApp Business API.** For instant confirmation, location pin, and reminder messaging. This single addition reduces no-shows by 15–25%.
- **Dialler with callback queue.** Missed calls get auto-queued for callback within an SLA (15 minutes for OPD enquiries, 5 minutes for emergencies routed to information line).
- **Dashboard.** Real-time visibility on calls handled, missed, conversion rate, average handle time.

Investment: roughly ₹4–8 lakh setup, ₹30,000–80,000/month ongoing for a mid-sized hospital. Payback inside the first quarter for almost every hospital we've worked with.

## The KPI Tree

What you measure is what improves. The funnel to track, weekly:

- **Pickup rate** — % of incoming calls answered within 3 rings. Target: > 95%.
- **Qualification rate** — % of picked-up calls that are genuine enquiries (vs. wrong numbers, internal calls). Target: > 70%.
- **First-call resolution rate** — % of qualified calls resolved without callback. Target: > 80%.
- **Appointment-booked rate** — % of qualified calls ending with a confirmed slot. Target: > 60%.
- **Show-up rate** — % of booked appointments that actually arrive. Target: > 75% with WhatsApp reminders.
- **OPD-to-IPD conversion** — % of OPD consults that result in a procedure or admission within 30 days. Tracked separately by specialty.

Multiply through: pickup × qualification × first-call × appointment × show-up. A hospital at 95% × 70% × 80% × 60% × 75% = 24% end-to-end conversion from raw calls to OPD show-ups. That's the benchmark.

If your number is below 18%, the call centre is leaking. If it's above 30%, you're best-in-class — share what you're doing with the rest of the org.

## The Escalation Triangle

Not every call should go to a generalist agent. Build a tiered structure:

- **Tier 1 — General agents:** OPD bookings, basic queries, routine specialty matching. Roughly 80% of call volume.
- **Tier 2 — Specialty coordinators:** Complex cases requiring clinical context — second opinions, multi-specialty referrals, IPD planning, high-value international enquiries. Cardiology, oncology and transplant should each have a dedicated coordinator.
- **Tier 3 — IPD desk / financial counsellor:** Patients moving toward admission, insurance pre-authorisation, cost estimates, package consultations.

Escalation rules: any caller mentioning cancer, transplant, cardiac surgery, paediatric specialty, or international travel auto-routes to Tier 2 after Act 1. Patients with insurance/cost questions route to Tier 3 after Act 2.

> The mistake: hiding the specialty coordinators behind the general agents. The right model is for the general agent to do the qualification and warm-transfer the call live, on the same call, to the right coordinator. No callbacks.

## The Marketing Loop

The call centre is also the highest-fidelity feedback channel in your marketing operation. Every call has a campaign source if your tracking is set up properly. Weekly:

- Which campaigns drove the most calls?
- Which campaigns drove the most *qualified* calls?
- Which keywords or creative angles correlated with higher booking rates?
- What questions are agents fielding repeatedly? Those questions should become content on the website.

A hospital that closes this loop — call centre data flowing back into marketing — typically improves ROAS on [paid campaigns](/paid) by 20–30% within two quarters. The conversion lever isn't in the ad; it's in matching the ad promise to what the call centre can deliver.
