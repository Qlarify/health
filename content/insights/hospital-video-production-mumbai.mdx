Mumbai is India's largest hospital market by revenue — and its most competitive for hospital video production. This guide covers what CMOs and marketing heads at Mumbai hospitals need to know before commissioning a video partner in 2026.

## Mumbai's hospital market

Mumbai's private hospital market includes some of India's most well-known hospital brands: Kokilaben Dhirubhai Ambani Hospital, Wockhardt Hospitals, Breach Candy Hospital, Lilavati Hospital, Hinduja Hospital, and Fortis Hiranandani — alongside smaller specialist centres across the Western suburbs, Thane, and Navi Mumbai.

The market has several structural features that shape how hospital video production needs to work here:

**Multilingual patient base.** Mumbai's patient population speaks primarily Hindi, Marathi, and English — with Gujarati and Urdu as significant secondary languages. A hospital video strategy that produces only in English misses a substantial portion of the addressable patient base, particularly for OPD campaigns targeting residential catchment areas.

**Highest general agency competition.** Mumbai has more general video production agencies than any other Indian city. Several of them — including Honeycomb Creative Works, which has a Mumbai office — actively market to hospitals. The challenge is that none of them are hospital-only. They serve technology companies and consumer brands alongside healthcare, with no dedicated NMC compliance workflow.

**Premium expectations.** Mumbai's hospital market is the most price-tolerant in India for quality video production. Hospitals like Kokilaben compete on premium brand positioning — the production values expected here are higher than in secondary markets, and the compliance risk is higher given the market's visibility.

## Hindi and Marathi language video requirements

A hospital video strategy for Mumbai that doesn't account for Hindi and Marathi is missing a majority of the addressable patient base.

**Hindi video production** is the highest priority for hospitals serving catchment areas across Andheri, Malad, Kandivali, Thane, and Navi Mumbai — where the patient base includes a large Hindi-speaking population from North India and migrant communities.

**Marathi video production** is critical for hospitals serving the traditional residential areas of Mumbai — Dadar, Shivaji Park, Girgaon, Byculla, and the greater Konkan-origin community — where Marathi is the primary language. It is also the primary language for a large portion of the patient base in Navi Mumbai's planned sectors.

The opportunity is most visible in the OPD campaign context: a Marathi-language diabetes awareness OPD campaign video, running on Meta in the Dadar and Shivaji Park geographic target, faces zero creative competition from any other hospital in Mumbai. No hospital currently produces this content.

## Why general Mumbai video agencies fail on hospital briefs

Mumbai's general video production industry is sophisticated and technically capable. The failure isn't in production quality — it's in specialisation.

**NMC compliance is absent from most general agency workflows.** NMC ethical guidelines restrict absolute claims, require verifiable credentials, demand written consent for patient testimonials, and prohibit specific superlative language about outcomes. A general agency scriptwriter who doesn't know these guidelines will write scripts that violate them, creating compliance liability for the hospital — not the agency.

**Patient psychology by specialty is not understood.** The anxiety a cardiac patient feels before bypass surgery is fundamentally different from the anxiety an orthopaedic patient feels before knee replacement. A general agency scriptwriter approaches both as a "patient story" brief. A hospital-specialist writer understands which specific fears need to be addressed in which order for each specialty, because those fears determine whether the patient converts or doesn't call.

**Language coordination is patchwork.** General agencies handle Hindi and Marathi as a voiceover add-on, often using freelance talent with no medical scripting background. Hospital-specialist agencies produce Hindi and Marathi scripts from the source — with medical accuracy checked against the English script at scripting stage, not post-production.
