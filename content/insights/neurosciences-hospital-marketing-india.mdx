Neurosciences sits in an odd corner of the Indian hospital service-line mix. It is one of the highest-revenue and most referral-dependent specialties on paper, yet it is rarely marketed with the same intent as cardiology or oncology. The reason is partly cultural — patients and GPs still treat neurology as a "wait and see" specialty — and partly structural: the patient journey is long, branching, and time-critical all at once.

Stroke is a 4.5-hour window. Epilepsy is a 30-year relationship. Back pain crosses ortho and neuro and gets lost in the handoff. The playbook for growing a neurosciences service line in a Bengaluru, Mumbai, or Delhi NCR hospital does not look like the playbook for cardiology, and treating them the same is how units underperform their bed-allocation.

This is field notes for marketers, service-line heads, and CMOs building or repositioning a neurosciences vertical. It assumes you already have the clinical team and equipment story sorted. The question is how to fill the OPD chairs and the OT slots.

## The neurology patient journey is non-linear

Most neurosciences patients do not arrive because they searched "best neurologist near me". They arrive because something has been wrong for months — sometimes years — and a tipping-point event made them act.

A typical journey looks like:

- **Symptom denial** — recurrent headaches, tingling in the fingers, dizziness, brief blackouts, memory slips. Patients chalk it up to stress, blood sugar, dehydration, or "just age".
- **GP or local physician** — a paracetamol prescription, a vitamin B12 panel, maybe an MRI order if the GP is conservative.
- **First neurology consult** — often months in, sometimes only after an emergency event.
- **Diagnosis** — and here the path forks dramatically. Migraine and tension headache go one way. A pituitary adenoma goes another. An early Parkinson's diagnosis means a 20-year care relationship.
- **Treatment path** — medication management, neurosurgical intervention, rehab, or all three.

The marketing implication: you are not targeting "neurology patients" as a single audience. You are running three or four parallel campaigns at different points in the same journey.

> If you only show up at the bottom of the funnel — paid search for "best neurosurgeon" — you are competing for the 5% of journeys that resolve in a transactional consult. The other 95% is won by whichever hospital owned the awareness stage.

## Channel strategy by sub-specialty

Neurosciences is not one service line. It is at least six, and each has its own channel logic.

### Stroke

Stroke is the only neuro emergency the lay public half-understands, and even then **FAST** awareness in India is patchy. The marketing job is twofold: build instinctive recognition of stroke symptoms, and ensure your hospital is the reflex destination when those symptoms hit.

- **Paid search**: Always-on campaigns for "stroke hospital near me", "best stroke treatment Bengaluru", "thrombolysis hospital Mumbai". The intent here is hot, the timing is now, and the CPL is justified.
- **YouTube**: 60-90 second stroke awareness clips featuring a senior neurologist demonstrating BE-FAST. Localised — show a familiar setting, speak in the regional language version, end with a single ER number.
- **Stroke hotline positioning**: Apollo and Manipal have invested heavily in "code stroke" hotlines and time-to-needle metrics. If you have the clinical capability, market it. If you do not, build it before you market it.

See our [video marketing playbook](/video) for production specs on emergency-response creative.

### Epilepsy and movement disorders

Long-term conditions. The marketing job is to be the hospital the patient stays with for two decades, not the one they consult once.

- **SEO**: Condition-deep content — "epilepsy in children", "Parkinson's stages explained", "essential tremor vs Parkinson's". This is the bread-and-butter of [hospital SEO](/seo) for a movement-disorder programme.
- **YouTube**: Patient-and-caregiver explainers. A neurologist walking through DBS (deep brain stimulation) decision points. An epileptologist discussing AED side-effects honestly.
- **Email and WhatsApp nurture**: This is where chronic-care service lines actually win. A diagnosed Parkinson's patient should hear from the hospital every 60-90 days with substantive content, not promotions. See our [email and lifecycle](/email) approach.

### Headache and migraine

The under-marketed gateway. Headache clinics convert into broader neurosciences relationships because patients who trust you with their headaches will trust you with their child's first seizure.

- **Social and content**: "When is a headache serious?" performs every time. Cluster headache, hemicrania continua, secondary headache red flags. Patients consume this voraciously.
- **The trap**: do not promise relief. ASCI rules prohibit guarantees, and clinically there is no honest guarantee in headache medicine.

### Spine and neurosurgery

Spine surgery sits between orthopaedics and neurosurgery, and the cross-referral mess inside hospitals is reflected in confused marketing.

- **Decision content**: "Surgery vs physiotherapy for slipped disc", "when is spine surgery actually necessary". Patients are terrified of spine surgery. Honesty here builds more trust than any testimonial campaign.
- **Robotic neurosurgery positioning**: Mazor X, ROSA, and other platforms are real differentiators if you have them. But the positioning has to be precision-and-recovery, not novelty. NMC and ASCI both look unkindly at "robotic = better" framing without clinical context.

### Neuro-oncology

Cross-references the [oncology service line](/specialties/oncology). Brain tumour patients shop hospitals harder than almost any other cohort because the stakes are absolute and the second opinions are mandatory.

- The marketing is multidisciplinary tumour board positioning, not single-surgeon promotion.

### Paediatric neurology

Smallest sub-segment, longest LTV. A child diagnosed with epilepsy at 6 is a 40-year care relationship if you are the family's trusted hospital.

## The referral network is half the game

In neurosciences more than almost any other specialty, the GP, paediatrician, and physician referral network drives volume. A Chennai hospital that wins the trust of 200 local GPs across the city will outpace a hospital with twice the digital spend.

- **Referral CME programmes**: Quarterly sessions in suburbs where your referring physicians actually practise. Substantive content, not branded fluff.
- **One-page referral guides**: When does a headache patient need an urgent MRI? When does a tingling patient need an EMG? GPs want clear rules.
- **Closed-loop feedback**: When a GP refers a patient, the GP gets a clinical note within 48 hours. This is operational, not marketing — but the operational gap is the marketing problem.
- **Digital-physical integration**: Your [paid](/paid) campaigns drive direct OPD, but your field force enables the chronic-care referrals that fund the unit.

## The awareness day calendar

A neurosciences content calendar has more anchoring days than most service lines. Plan around them, but do not over-rely:

- **World Stroke Day** — October 29
- **World Brain Day** — July 22
- **National Epilepsy Day** — November 17 (India-specific)
- **International Epilepsy Day** — second Monday of February
- **Parkinson's Awareness Month** — April
- **Migraine Awareness Week** — varies, June
- **Alzheimer's Awareness Month** — September

For each, plan a content tentpole: a doctor-led YouTube video, an SEO article that targets a non-obvious long-tail query, and a [social](/social) sequence that runs the week before and the week of. The mistake is doing all of them — pick three the unit owns and go deep, instead of seven shallow posts.

## KPIs that actually matter

Neurosciences marketing is measured wrong in most Indian hospitals. Generic "leads" and "form fills" mask the unit economics. The metrics that actually matter:

- **Cost per neurology OPD consult** — usually ₹400-1,200 depending on geography and channel mix. Bengaluru and Delhi NCR are higher than tier-2.
- **Cost per neurosurgery OPD consult** — significantly higher, ₹2,000-6,000, justified by case value.
- **OPD-to-imaging conversion** — what percentage of neuro OPDs convert to in-house MRI or EEG. This is where the service line actually monetises.
- **Imaging-to-procedure conversion** — for surgical pathways, what percentage of MRIs convert to surgical consult.
- **Stroke door-to-needle time** — clinical, but marketable. Your time-to-needle is your strongest single asset for emergency positioning.
- **Referral-source attribution** — what share of OPDs come from GP referrals vs. digital vs. walk-in vs. internal cross-referral.

## Compliance, briefly

Neurosciences is regulatorily sensitive in three places:

- **No outcome guarantees** — ASCI prohibits "100% recovery" and similar claims. This includes stroke and epilepsy.
- **NMC advertising code** — individual doctor-led promotion has clear limits. Hospital-led promotion is broader, but doctor names and qualifications must be accurate and uninflated.
- **DPDP Act, 2023** — neurological conditions are sensitive personal data. Any lead capture that mentions specific conditions (epilepsy, dementia, stroke history) needs explicit, granular consent. WhatsApp nurture flows for chronic neuro patients need especially careful consent design.

## The 90-day starter plan

If you are reading this and you run marketing for a neurosciences unit that has been under-performing, here is the compressed version:

1. **Audit the OPD attribution** — where are your current consults coming from? You probably do not know precisely, and that is the first fix.
2. **Pick two sub-specialties to lead with** — usually stroke (emergency, high-margin) and one chronic-care vertical (epilepsy or movement disorders).
3. **Build the foundational content** — 12 YouTube videos, 20 SEO articles, 6 referral CME modules. This is the asset base that compounds.
4. **Turn on always-on stroke paid search** — the unit-economics work even at high CPLs because case values are high.
5. **Set up GP referral tracking** — even a manual spreadsheet is better than the nothing most units run on.
6. **Measure monthly, not quarterly** — neurosciences marketing has long latencies in chronic care, but short feedback loops in emergencies. You need both views.

The hospitals that win the next five years in Indian neurosciences will not be the ones with the most expensive robot. They will be the ones whose name a Mumbai or Hyderabad family thinks of in the four-and-a-half-hour window after a parent slurs their words. That recall is built years before the event — in the headache video they watched, the epilepsy article their GP shared, the brain-day reel a cousin saw.
