A patient in Whitefield wakes up at 2am with chest tightness. Before she calls anyone, she opens ChatGPT and types: *"is left arm pain a heart attack symptom and which hospital in Bengaluru is best for cardiac emergency"*. Ninety seconds later, she has a triage answer, three hospital names, and an Uber booking. None of that journey touched a Google blue link.

This is not a future scenario. It's the median patient research journey in metro India by mid-2026. **A meaningful share of healthcare research now happens inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude and Gemini — not on the SERP.** The hospital that wins the citation inside the AI answer wins the patient. AEO/GEO is the discipline of being that citation.

If your hospital's [SEO strategy](/seo) still ends at "rank on page one of Google," you're optimising for a shrinking surface. The patient flow that used to land on your page from a SERP click increasingly lands as a single line in an AI answer — with or without a citation link. Either you're the source it cites, or you're invisible.

## The Vocabulary: AEO, GEO, AISO

Three acronyms describe the same discipline from different angles:

- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimisation)** — the older term, originally about featured snippets and "position zero" on Google. Now used broadly for optimising any content to be extracted as the direct answer.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)** — coined in 2023–24 academic literature, specifically about getting content cited inside generative AI responses (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini).
- **AISO (AI Search Optimisation)** — the umbrella term some agencies now prefer.

Vendors will argue about which is "correct." Don't waste time on it. The discipline is the same: structure your content so an LLM, when asked a healthcare question grounded in real-time retrieval, picks your page as the source and lifts your sentence into its answer.

## Why YMYL Healthcare Is the Most Filtered Category

Healthcare is **YMYL** — "Your Money or Your Life" content, in Google's E-E-A-T framework. Every major AI engine applies heavier filters here than to almost any other category. Perplexity routes medical queries through curated medical sources first. ChatGPT's browsing layer prioritises domains with explicit clinical bylines. Google's AI Overviews shows reduced surfacing for medical queries from low-authority domains, and explicit "consult a doctor" disclaimers.

This is good news for hospitals — *if* you set up the content correctly. The filter excludes the affiliate-spam tier, leaving real clinical authorities competing for the citation slot. It's bad news for any hospital still publishing AI-generated content with a fake "Dr. Smith" byline. Those pages aren't ranking on Google and they aren't getting cited by ChatGPT.

## The Five Signals AI Engines Lean On

After auditing roughly 200 hospital content pieces against actual AI citation outcomes (Perplexity referrers, ChatGPT browsing logs where available, manual prompt testing), five signals dominate which hospital pages get cited in AI answers:

### 1. E-E-A-T with a Named Clinician

The single highest-correlation signal. AI engines prefer pages where the byline is a credentialled, verifiable doctor — not "Editorial Team" or "Marketing Department." A page reviewed by **Dr. Priya Rao, MD (Cardiology), Manipal Hospital Whitefield** with a link to her hospital profile gets cited more often than the same content under a generic byline.

This is also why the [doctor personal branding](/contact) angle compounds — credentialled doctor pages become AI citation magnets.

### 2. Structured Data — Especially FAQPage and MedicalEntity

Schema.org markup is the difference between "AI engine has to guess what this page is about" and "AI engine sees this is a fact, here is the question, here is the answer." For hospitals, three schema types matter:

- **FAQPage** — the single highest-extraction format. Every clinical content piece should end with 5–8 FAQ entries.
- **MedicalEntity / MedicalCondition / MedicalProcedure** — used sparingly because they're complex, but high-value for procedure pages.
- **Organization with medicalSpecialty** — for hospital and department pages.

> The pattern that works: write the article, end with an FAQPage block of the 6 questions a patient actually asks before booking. Use full-sentence answers. Don't truncate.

### 3. Explicit Jurisdiction Signals

AI engines are trying to match the patient's likely location to the content. "Best cardiac hospital" pulls different answers in India vs the US. If your content is for Indian patients, *say so explicitly* — in the title, in the first paragraph, in the schema. Reference **NABH accreditation, NMC compliance, [DPDP Act](/glossary)** where relevant. Use Indian city names. Quote prices in INR.

A page that says "cardiac bypass surgery in India typically costs ₹2.5–4.5 lakh at NABH-accredited hospitals" will be cited for India queries. A page that says "cardiac bypass surgery costs $30,000–$50,000" will not.

### 4. Citation by Other Authoritative Sources

The web-of-trust signal. If your page is referenced by a peer-reviewed paper, a *Times of India* health article, an NIH-indexed publication, or a credentialled doctor's own site, AI engines weight you higher. This is the long-game version of digital PR.

### 5. Freshness

Healthcare facts change. AI engines penalise stale content — especially in oncology, where treatment protocols shift annually. **Date-stamp every clinical page. Update at least annually.** Add a "Last reviewed by Dr. X on [date]" footer. We've measured 3–5x higher citation rates for content updated in the last 12 months versus content older than 24 months.

## FAQPage Schema: The Highest-Extraction Format

If you do one thing this quarter, add FAQPage schema to your top 20 service-line pages. The format that works:

```
Q: How long is recovery after coronary bypass surgery in India?
A: Most patients in Indian NABH-accredited cardiac hospitals are discharged 5–7 days after coronary artery bypass surgery and return to light activities by 4–6 weeks. Full recovery, including return to demanding physical work, typically takes 8–12 weeks. Cardiac rehabilitation programmes during this period improve outcomes.
```

Three things make that answer extractable:

1. **Complete sentence.** Not "5–7 days." A complete sentence the AI engine can lift wholesale.
2. **Context.** "in Indian NABH-accredited cardiac hospitals." This is the jurisdiction signal in line one.
3. **Specificity.** Numbers, ranges, time horizons. Vague answers get filtered out.

Avoid the question patterns that don't extract well: yes/no questions, "what is X" where X is obvious from context, anything that requires the patient to already know clinical terminology.

## The Named Clinician Byline Pattern

Every healthcare content piece should be **authored or reviewed** by a credentialled doctor with a verifiable profile on your site. This is non-negotiable in 2026 — both for AI citation and for ASCI compliance.

The structure that works:

- **Author:** the marketing or editorial team member who wrote the prose
- **Medically reviewed by:** the credentialled doctor, with name, qualification, registration number, and link to hospital profile
- **Last reviewed:** date

For procedure pages, the reviewer should be a doctor who *actually performs that procedure* at your hospital. AI engines have started cross-referencing — they notice when "Dr. X, Cardiologist" reviews a dental implant article.

## llms.txt: The Emerging Robots.txt for AI

In 2024–25, a community standard emerged for `llms.txt` — a file at the root of your domain (like `robots.txt`) that tells AI crawlers what they can index, in what format, and where to find clean structured content.

For hospitals, a minimal `llms.txt` should:

- List the canonical entry points for each specialty
- Indicate the FAQ corpus location
- Mark sensitive pages (patient stories with consent issues) as do-not-index
- Provide a contact for AI partnerships

Adoption among AI engines is still partial — Anthropic and a handful of others honour it; OpenAI's behaviour is less consistent. But the cost to deploy is one afternoon, and the upside is meaningful.

## Tracking AI Citation: It's Harder Than SEO

Here's the operational reality: you cannot get clean attribution from AI engines the way you can from Google Analytics. The signals you can use:

- **Perplexity referrers** — Perplexity passes a referrer header for clicks from citations. Filter your analytics for `perplexity.ai` referrers.
- **ChatGPT referrers** — ChatGPT's browsing layer also passes referrers, though less consistently. Filter for `chat.openai.com` and `chatgpt.com`.
- **Direct testing** — manually prompt ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini with the top 50 queries you care about. Record which sources they cite. Run quarterly.
- **Brand mention monitoring** — Common Room, Brand24, or manual tracking of when AI engines mention your hospital by name.
- **Indirect signal** — anomalous direct-traffic spikes to specific deep pages often correspond to AI citation events.

> The hospitals with the best AEO operations have a quarterly "AI audit" — prompt the major engines with their target query set and grade each response on whether they were cited.

## Common Mistakes Hospitals Make

The errors we see repeatedly:

- **Keyword-stuffing for AI prompts.** AI engines don't reward density — they reward clarity. Write for the human reader.
- **Faking author bylines.** Generated personas with stock photos get filtered. AI engines now cross-reference NMC registration databases.
- **Gaming testimonials.** Don't fabricate patient stories. ASCI now treats AI-generated testimonials as deceptive advertising.
- **One-time publish, never update.** Healthcare content has a half-life. Stale pages lose citation share within 12–18 months.
- **No FAQPage schema.** Still the single highest-leverage missing element on most hospital sites we audit.
- **Optimising for the wrong query.** Patients don't ask "best multispecialty hospital Bengaluru." They ask "where should I take my mother for kidney transplant evaluation in Bengaluru." Match the conversational query, not the bidding term.

## The Six-Month Operating Plan

If you're starting from zero, here's the sequence:

**Month 1–2:** Audit your top 50 service-line pages. Add named clinician reviewers. Date-stamp every page. Deploy FAQPage schema on the top 20.

**Month 3:** Build out the FAQ corpus — every specialty page gets 6–10 questions answered in complete-sentence format. Add explicit India context.

**Month 4:** Deploy `llms.txt`. Start the citation tracking practice — quarterly prompts against the top engines.

**Month 5–6:** Compound the credibility — get cited by news sources, peer publications. Update older content.

By end of the cycle, you should see your hospital starting to appear in AI answers for specialty and city-specific queries. The hospitals doing this in 2024–25 had a meaningful head start; the hospitals starting in late 2026 will be playing catch-up against entrenched citations.

This isn't a side project for the SEO team. It's a strategic shift in how patients find healthcare information. The discipline overlaps with traditional SEO — the foundations of E-E-A-T, structured data, and authoritative content are shared. But the optimisation surface is different, and the measurement is different. Treat it as a separate workstream with its own KPIs.
